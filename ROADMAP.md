# Roadmap and vision

AI Phone-In Studio started with one format: a human presenter hosting a show with fictional AI callers.

Building it has helped us understand the basic ingredients of a new kind of small, personal studio. There is a show structure, a host, a running order, different characters and voices, prepared visuals, live conversation and enough producer control to keep everything moving in the intended direction.

The phone-in is one expression of that. The same studio model could evolve into several others.

> This is a direction-setting document, not a fixed release schedule. The purpose of the working studio is to make these ideas testable and discover which of them have life in them.

## A better AI phone-in

The immediate path is to keep improving the original format: more natural callers, stronger characters, better voices and conversations that feel less turn-based.

When OpenAI's next Live voice capability becomes available through the API, lower latency and full-duplex conversation should make interruption, humour, hesitation and quicker exchanges feel much more natural. That may be the point where an AI caller stops feeling like a voice assistant taking turns and begins to feel present in a live programme.

## Interview practice

The same structure could become a mock interview studio.

A candidate could choose a role, company or interview style and be questioned by different interviewers with different voices and personalities. One might be encouraging, another more direct, and another focused on technical or situational questions. The session could then help the candidate review their answers, confidence and areas to improve.

This is still a hosted spoken format: characters arrive with a purpose, ask questions, react to the person and move the session forward. It simply replaces the entertainment phone-in with a useful rehearsal.

## A personal breakfast television show

The studio could also become a small breakfast television-style programme built around the news and conversations of the day.

A human presenter could move through news items, blog posts, social posts or other material, with visual characters appearing as contributors, guests or experts where appropriate. Different voices could bring different perspectives, explain a subject or challenge what the presenter has said. Images, headlines and source material could appear on screen while the conversation continues.

This would not need a conventional television production team. One person could shape and present the programme while AI helps populate the studio and assemble the material around them.

## Turning a personal feed into a programme

Instead of scrolling through a social feed or list of updates, the same information could be turned into a short radio or television show.

A local football club might have a programme covering results, fixtures, team news, supporter posts and upcoming events. A community, business sector or interest group could have its own regular update. The content already exists; the difference is that it is selected, voiced and presented as a programme rather than left as a stream of disconnected posts.

These shows could be personal to the viewer or made for a small shared audience. They could be generated when requested, published on a schedule, or hosted live by a person who wants to add their own judgement and personality.

## Exploring the frontier

The wider idea is to explore how far spoken voice, live conversation, visual contributors and lightweight production tools can take personal broadcasting.

The working phone-in studio gives us somewhere to try these formats rather than only imagine them. We can evolve the original show, prototype interview practice, build a breakfast-style programme and test what happens when a feed becomes something you watch or listen to.

As voice latency and quality improve, those experiments should become more conversational, responsive and believable. The next step is not to decide today which one becomes the final product. It is to use the studio to make a few of them real, put them in front of people and discover which directions have life in them.
